# Prudential — Life Insurance (#5 Worst — 2.5/5)

> **Industry:** Insurance / Life | **Type:** Agent + Paramedical Exam | **Rating:** 2.5/5 | **Date:** 2026-08-05

---

## The Most Invasive Consumer Onboarding in Any Industry

| Step | What Happens | Time |
|------|-------------|------|
| 1 | Meet with agent. Discuss needs. Fill health questionnaire (family history, lifestyle, medications, travel) | 1-2 hours |
| 2 | Schedule paramedical exam. Nurse comes to your home/office | 1-2 weeks wait |
| 3 | Blood draw, urine sample, height/weight, blood pressure, EKG (age-dependent) | 30-45 min |
| 4 | Lab results processed. MIB (Medical Information Bureau) check. APS (Attending Physician Statement) if flagged | 2-4 weeks |
| 5 | Underwriter reviews: lab results + MIB + health questionnaire + APS + motor vehicle report + prescription history | 1-3 weeks |
| 6 | Decision: standard, preferred, rated (substandard), or declined | Total: **3-6 weeks** |

**Compare with Lemonade:** 90 seconds. AI chatbot. Policy issued.

**Compare with Assurance IQ (Prudential's own digital subsidiary):** Term life up to $1.5M with NO medical exam. Algorithmic underwriting using MIB + Rx check + MVR + public records. 15-20 minutes. Prudential KNOWS this model works — they acquired Assurance IQ for $2.35B in 2019. But the core Prudential product still requires a blood draw.

This is the perfect example of "adding a digital layer on top of an analog process" instead of rebuilding the process. Prudential bought a digital company instead of digitizing itself.

**Lesson for QBO:** Don't buy a modern product and bolt it onto a legacy product. Rebuild the core.
