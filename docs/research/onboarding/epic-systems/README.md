# Epic Systems — Healthcare EHR (#1 Worst — 2.0/5)

> **Industry:** Healthcare / EHR | **Type:** Enterprise Hospital Implementation | **Rating:** 2.0/5 | **Date:** 2026-08-05

---

## Why It's the Worst Onboarding in This Research

Epic is the dominant US electronic health record (250M+ patient records). Hospitals spend **$50M-$500M+ and 12-24 months** implementing it. There is no "sign up." There is no "self-serve." There is a contract, an army of consultants, and years of configuration.

### Concrete Example: What "Onboarding" Looks Like
| Phase | What Happens | Time |
|-------|-------------|------|
| **Contract** | Hospital signs multi-year contract with Epic. $50M-$500M+ depending on hospital size | 3-6 months |
| **Planning** | Epic implementation team arrives on-site. Maps every department (ER, ICU, surgery, outpatient, pharmacy, lab, billing) | 3-6 months |
| **Configuration** | EpicCare modules configured per department. Workflows customized. Billing/RCM integrated. MyChart patient portal set up. Role-based access (physician, nurse, admin, pharmacist) configured | 6-12 months |
| **Training** | Every doctor, nurse, and staff member trained on Epic. Some resist (physicians are notorious for hating EHR training) | 2-4 months |
| **Go Live** | Epic goes live. Celebrated as a milestone. Issues surface. Consultants remain on-site for months | Ongoing |
| **Total** | | **12-24 months** |

### Why It's Broken — And Why Epic Doesn't Care
| Problem | Root Cause | Lesson for QBO |
|---------|-----------|---------------|
| No self-serve onboarding | Epic's market is hospitals, not individuals. Every implementation is bespoke | Even enterprise products need a self-serve trial path |
| 12-24 month time-to-value | Healthcare regulations require massive configuration | Break complex products into independently deployable modules |
| Zero UX investment | Epic has no incentive — switching costs are astronomical | Never let switching costs become your primary retention strategy |
| Credentialing as onboarding | Doctor credentialing (NPI, DEA, license verification) takes 90-120 days | Automate credential verification. Defer non-critical checks |
| Monopolistic behavior | Epic dominates large hospitals. Only Cerner competes meaningfully | QBO has 80% SMB share. Danger zone: market dominance → UX complacency |
